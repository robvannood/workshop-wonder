# Tinkering World — Design Constitution

## Purpose
Create a playable experiential self-portrait of Rob van Nood's beliefs about learning. The game should let players feel that understanding is built through encounter, curiosity, resistance, tinkering, recognition, creation, identity, and contribution.

## Experience principles
- Discovery before instruction.
- Direct manipulation before abstract controls.
- Failure is physical information, not punishment.
- Help preserves ownership and never gives the complete solution.
- Solitude can coexist with belonging through traces of previous makers and contribution to a larger system.
- Completion is not a score or reward screen; it is evidence that the player's work matters beyond the immediate object.

## Visual principles
- Handmade, tactile, textured, real, well lit, and quietly magical.
- Cardboard is a signature material, alongside worn wood, aged paper, thread, screws, clamps, and modest metal parts.
- The room should feel inhabited but currently empty: repaired joints, crossed-out sketches, taped notes, stains, tool marks, and unfinished attempts.
- Avoid flat educational diagrams, corporate interfaces, polished steampunk, horror, and muddy brown uniformity.

## Audio principles
- A toy workshop, not a clock shop.
- Use wood, paper, cardboard, thread, small metal contacts, room tone, and sparse resonant sounds.
- Synchronize sound with visible contact and energy transfer.
- No generic success jingle or continuous soundtrack.

## Vertical-slice boundaries
- One room.
- One central kinetic installation.
- Approximately 8–12 minutes for a first-time player.
- At least three meaningful direct manipulations.
- At least two believable, recoverable failure states.
- One optional, one-use Question Card.
- A concluding response from beyond the immediate mechanism.

## Prohibited drift
Do not add combat, points, inventory, dialogue trees, conventional tutorial text, multiple rooms, or additional mechanisms until the core interaction is tactile, legible, stable, and emotionally coherent.
